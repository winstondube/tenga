Three ways to show the shops

The job of this section is two things at once: you will recognise these names, and you are not restricted to them. The current version does the first with a row of tags and the second with a sentence, which puts the reassurance and the important part in the wrong order.

Current

A row of tags

Eleven mono chips read as filters you might click, the eye scans them as a menu, and the one thing that matters, that any shop works, is buried in a paragraph above.

Shops we know well We buy from anywhere in the UK

These are the shops we order from most, so we already have their delivery rules and quirks written down. You are not limited to them. Send a link from any UK shop and we will price it.

Option A

Say the important thing loudly, list the shops quietly

The headline carries the message and the shop names drop into prose underneath, where they belong. No chips, nothing that looks clickable, and the sentence people need to read is the biggest thing on screen. This is the smallest change and the least likely to date.

Any UK shop.
Not a list to pick from.

Boots, Superdrug, LookFantastic, Cult Beauty, Beauty Bay, Space NK, ASOS, Next, Afro Hair & Beauty, Sephora UK.

Those are the ten we order from most, so their delivery rules and quirks are already written down and your quote comes back faster. Everywhere else works exactly the same way, we just confirm the postage with them before we price it.

Option B

Show the delivery rules, since we claim to have them

The current copy says "we already have their delivery rules written down" and then shows none of them. This shows them. It turns decoration into something a customer can actually use to decide what to order and from where, and the last row makes the any-shop point without a paragraph. It is the only version that gets more useful as you add shops.

Option C

Show a link being accepted, over and over

An address bar types out a real product URL, from a different shop each time, and confirms it. It demonstrates the promise instead of asserting it, and every few seconds it quietly proves it again with a shop nobody expected. Riskier: it is motion in a section that does not strictly need it, and it repeats the hero's input.
