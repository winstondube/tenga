Three ways to make the hero move

Same headline, same photographs, same price arithmetic in all three. Only the motion differs. Each one loops; press replay to watch it again from the start. All of them stop entirely if your system is set to reduce motion.

Concept A

Three orders, fulfilled in front of you

Your idea. Three real-looking orders run through the whole lifecycle, link to landed, one after another. It answers "what is this?" without a word of explanation, and it quietly proves the thing people doubt: that parcels actually arrive. The risk is that it is busy, and that a visitor has to wait to see the end.

Concept B

The price assembling itself

My first. Nothing moves except the number. The photograph drifts almost imperceptibly, and the price tag builds one line at a time until the total lands and gets stamped confirmed. It makes one argument rather than five: you will know the whole cost before you pay. Quietest of the three, and the least likely to feel like a banner ad.

Concept C

A shelf going past, one thing picked off it

My second, and the most literal reading of the headline. Rows of UK products drift past like shelves. Every few seconds one is lifted out, given a price, and flown off to the right. It sells range rather than a single perfume, which matters now that you buy from any shop, and it needs no reading at all. The risk is that it is the busiest of the three and leans decorative.
